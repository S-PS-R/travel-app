# Naming direction

## Recommended: Veyfar / Veyfarer

Pronunciation: VAY-far; member identity: VAY-far-er.

“You’re a Veyfarer, right?”

A modern coined brand inspired by Old Norse **vegr** (way/road), **fara** (go/travel), and the familiar sound of “wayfarer.” It is not presented as a historical Norse word or a literal translation. Short, readable, and suited to travelers on foot, by sea, by rail, or by air. Applied as the working display name; existing storage identifiers and URL scheme stay compatible with the first version.

- [University of Texas, Old Norse lexicon: vegr](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/eieol_base_form_dictionary/norol/18)
- [Cleasby–Vigfusson dictionary entry: fara](https://oldnorsedictionary.com/home/word/fara)

## Indian-inspired alternative: Pathik / Pathiker

Inspired by Sanskrit **pathika**, a traveler or wanderer. “Pathik” has a direct human identity and a pleasing association with the English “path.” “Pathiker” would be our coined community name, not a Sanskrit grammatical form.

- [Sanskrit Dictionary: pathika](https://sanskritdictionary.com/?action=Search&iencoding=iast&lang=sans&q=pathika)

## Greek-inspired alternative: Nost / Noster

A short coined brand from Greek **nostos**, homecoming, a central idea in the Odyssey. Particularly suited to preserving journeys and memories; less immediately suggestive of exploring than Veyfar.

- [Open University: Homecoming and reunion](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/exploring-homers-odyssey/content-section-6)

These directions share the traveler archetype across cultures without claiming the cultures used one name. Veyfar is the best fit for the requested conversational “app-name-er” identity. This is a creative shortlist, not a domain or trademark availability determination.
